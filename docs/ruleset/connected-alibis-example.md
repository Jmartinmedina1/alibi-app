# Connected alibis: one paper example

Paper only: no gameplay changes. The Riverside Fair reports are preserved; this is a new mechanism, not a revision of those.

## 1. Hidden facts (fixed before anything else)

**Case:** At 9:20 the baking trophy went missing from the judges' tent. Everyone accounts for **9:05–9:30**. Exactly one alibi is fabricated, and everyone knows this rule.

| Suspect | What really happened |
|---|---|
| **Granny Dot** (honest) | 9:05 collected the raffle box from Bruno at his food truck. 9:10–9:30 sold raffle tickets at the raffle table, the whole time. |
| **Skater Leo** (honest) | Until 9:12, skate demo at the ramp. 9:15 signed out the microphone from the stage manager. From 9:18, at the raffle table to announce the draw. |
| **Chef Bruno** (the Liar) | 9:02 made a sale at his truck. 9:05 gave Dot the raffle box. 9:10 left the truck and walked past Leo's skate demo. **9:12–9:25 in the judges' tent.** 9:27 made a sale back at the truck. |

**What Bruno knows:** his own timeline. That includes his register sales (9:02 and 9:27), the handoff to Dot, that Dot went to sell raffle tickets, and that Leo's demo was on at 9:10. He does **not** know about the ice delivery, or who was at the raffle table later.

**Evidence (contents fixed; titles public; contents shown only when opened):**

| | Source | Content | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Food-truck register | "Sales at 9:02 and 9:27. No sales in between." | Records sales only, not who was at the truck. |
| B | Raffle committee sheet | "Raffle box collected from the food truck by Granny Dot at 9:05. Tickets sold at the raffle table by Granny Dot, 9:10–9:30." | Names the seller, not the buyers. |
| C | Ice vendor's delivery log | "9:18: bag of ice for the food truck. Nobody there; left it by the door." | Covers only 9:18. |
| D | Stage manager's sheet | "Microphone signed out to Skater Leo at 9:15." | Shows who took it, not where it went. |

**Statements: each suspect locks one A/B/C/D alibi.** Honest suspects lock their true one:
- **Dot:** "I collected the raffle box from Bruno at his truck at 9:05, then sold raffle tickets at the raffle table from 9:10 to 9:30."
- **Leo:** "I did the skate demo until 9:12, signed out the microphone at 9:15, and was at the raffle table from 9:18 for the draw."

**Bruno's menu (all false):**

| | Alibi | True parts he keeps |
|---|---|---|
| A | "I gave Dot the raffle box at 9:05, then stayed at my truck restocking until 9:30." | The handoff; his truck sales at both ends |
| B | "I gave Dot the raffle box at 9:05, watched the end of Leo's skate demo, then bought raffle tickets at the raffle table around 9:20." | The handoff; the demo he really passed |
| C | "I gave Dot the raffle box at 9:05, then helped Leo carry the microphone from the stage." | The handoff |
| D | "I gave Dot the raffle box at 9:05, then went to the ice stand to buy ice." | The handoff |

**One follow-up question:** "Who did you see between 9:10 and 9:30?" Answers lock.
- **Dot:** "At the raffle table I saw only Leo, from 9:18."
- **Leo:** "Granny Dot at the raffle table from 9:18. Nobody else I know."
- **Bruno** picks the answer that matches his alibi: A "Nobody; I was at my truck." · B "Leo's skate crowd, then Dot at the raffle table." · C "Leo, at the stage." · D "The ice vendor."

**Turn menus:**
- **Each investigation:** A Open a record · B Ask a suspect · C Review statements · D Finish.
  - Records: A–D as in the evidence table.
  - Ask: A Dot · B Leo · C Bruno · D Back.
- **Budget:** two investigations, in any order.
- **Accusation:** A Dot · B Leo · C Bruno · D Insufficient evidence (a draw).

## 2. The two Liar options

| | Benefit | Exposure |
|---|---|---|
| **A: stayed at my truck** | Nobody else was at the truck, so no witness can contradict him. His register shows sales at both ends of the gap. | Something may show the truck empty during the gap. He doesn't know about the ice delivery, but he does know the register has a long gap. |
| **B: demo, then the raffle table** | Explains the register gap. Built on real events he knows (the demo, the raffle he supplied). Nothing about the truck can contradict it. | The raffle table had people at it. He knows Dot went there to sell, and Dot or anyone else there can contradict him. |

Neither is marked good or bad for the Detective. Each risks a different kind of check: witnesses for B, a hidden record for A.

## 3. Path 1: the investigation exposes the lie (Bruno chose B)

**Decision 1.**
- **Known:** three alibis. All three meet at the 9:05 handoff (Dot and Bruno) and at the raffle table around 9:18–9:20 (Dot, Leo and Bruno).
- **Choices:**
  - A: open a record. B (raffle sheet) would test Dot; A (register) or C (ice log) would test the truck, which nobody claims after 9:05.
  - B: ask someone.
- **Why ask:** the raffle table is where all three stories meet. Anyone who was there can test the others' claims at once.
- **Pick:** Ask Dot.
- **Result:** "At the raffle table I saw only Leo, from 9:18."
- **Effect:** Dot and Bruno can't both be right: he says he bought tickets there at 9:20. So the Liar is Dot or Bruno, and Leo is honest.

**Decision 2.**
- **Known:** Dot vs Bruno.
- **Choices:**
  - Raffle sheet: tests both parts of Dot's alibi.
  - Ask Leo: he is now known to be honest, and was at the table from 9:18.
  - Register: shows nothing about the raffle table.
- **Pick:** Raffle sheet. Asking Leo would also have settled it.
- **Result:** Dot collected the box at 9:05 and sold tickets 9:10–9:30. **Both parts of Dot's alibi are confirmed**, so hers isn't the fabricated one.
- **Accusation:** C, Bruno. **Correct.**

The true 9:05 handoff appears in both Dot's and Bruno's accounts. It was never treated as clearing Bruno.

## 4. Path 2: the cover survives (same facts; Bruno chose A)

**Decision 1.**
- **Known:** Dot's and Leo's alibis as before. Bruno: "gave Dot the box at 9:05, then stayed at my truck until 9:30."
- **Nothing conflicts:**
  - Bruno says he was at the truck; Dot and Leo were elsewhere.
  - The only shared event is the handoff, which both Dot and Bruno describe.
- **Choices:**
  - Raffle sheet: tests both parts of Dot's alibi.
  - Register: tests Bruno.
  - Stage sheet: tests part of Leo's.
  - A question: nobody was where anyone else claims to have been.
- **Why the raffle sheet:** it can confirm a whole alibi. Clearing one suspect leaves two.
- **Result:** Dot collected the box and sold tickets 9:10–9:30. Dot is honest.
- **Effect:** the Liar is Leo or Bruno. The sheet also confirms the 9:05 handoff in Bruno's story, but that is only the true part. It doesn't prove he stayed at the truck.

**Decision 2 (one investigation left).**
- **Known:** Leo vs Bruno.
  - **Leo:** his demo part has no record; the stage sheet can confirm only the sign-out.
  - **Bruno:** his truck claim can be tested by the register (named for the truck) or the ice log (relevance unknown from the title).
- **Choices:**
  - Register: the obvious record for "stayed at my truck".
  - Ice log: a gamble on an unclear title.
  - Stage sheet: only a detail of Leo's story.
  - Ask Leo or Bruno: neither was where the other claims to have been.
- **Pick:** Register.
- **Result:** "Sales at 9:02 and 9:27. No sales in between." That's consistent with restocking, which makes no sales. It neither confirms nor contradicts his presence.
- **Effect:** the register gap *would* make the ice log the next useful check, but the budget is spent.
- **Accusation:** D, insufficient evidence. **Draw: the cover survived.** No clear evidence was ignored.

**The same facts with different choices:** opening the ice log at decision 2 shows "9:18: nobody there", which contradicts "stayed at my truck until 9:30". Bruno is identified.

## 5. Weaknesses

1. **The ice log decides option A in one step if opened.** Its title doesn't say it concerns the truck, so opening it early is a gamble. When it lands, though, it's a single-record solution.
2. **Bruno is always the Liar here.** The Detective's uncertainty is real only on a first play. A single example can't show whether targeting Bruno's story is reasoning or luck; that needs versions where Dot or Leo could be the Liar.
3. **Leo's skate-demo part has no evidence at all.** If Leo were the Liar in another version, that part would be untestable. This example doesn't need it, but the pattern would.
4. **Option B may be weaker in practice.** A single natural question to anyone at the raffle table exposes it, while option A needs the less obvious ice log. How often each succeeds depends on how players choose, which I haven't assumed or measured.
5. **The two-investigation budget matters.** With three investigations, "register, then ice log" always catches option A after confirming Dot, which would make A weak too.
6. **Not run through the checker.** This is a hand-built example. Its logic was checked by hand only.
7. **Long sentences.** The accounts are full sentences; on a phone they may need trimming without losing the connected parts.
